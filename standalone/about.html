About Us

Here is a notion so often stitched onto cushions and printed under sunsets that we've rather stopped noticing it means anything at all: personal development is personal. Obvious, you'd think. And yet nearly everyone in the business hands you the identical package — the same affirmations, the same breezy calm, the same soothing voice that is emphatically not your own — as though one size could possibly fit the roughly eight billion wildly different people currently milling about the planet. It can't, of course. The part of you that makes you you is precisely the part no template can reach. Which is where we begin: with the frankly astonishing fact that human beings are creative beings. It is the great trick of our species — we picture something that isn't there, something that has never existed anywhere in the universe, and then, with considerable cheek, we go and make it real.

Consider, for a moment, the room you're sitting in. The chair beneath you, the device in your hand, the walls, the light switch, the improbably clever little phone that somehow knows where you are — every last bit of it was once nothing more than a flicker of electricity in somebody's head. Somebody thought it, and then it became a thing you could stub your toe on. This is so ordinary we never remark on it, and so extraordinary that it really ought to stop us in our tracks. Thought is the first draft of everything. And — this is the part that matters — a life is no exception. You are not stuck with the one you were handed. You are, whether you've quite noticed or not, already drafting the one you're becoming.

So the practice, mercifully, is simple — the sort of thing you could explain to a slightly distracted ten-year-old. See it: picture the person you intend to be, clearly, in your own words and nobody else's. Believe it: hold it as a fact you simply haven't caught up with yet, rather than a wish you're faintly embarrassed about. And feel it: borrow, in advance, the quiet joy and gratitude of the thing already done. See it, believe it, feel it — and repeat until it sticks. It is, as it happens, the oldest recipe there is for becoming who you meant to be all along. We just built the kitchen.
