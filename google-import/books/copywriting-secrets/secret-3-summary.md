# Copywriting Secrets — Secret 3: People Don't Buy Without a Reason Why

Source: user-pasted chapter (Jim Edwards quote)

## One-sentence summary
People buy for specific, repeatable reasons — identify multiple "whys" for your product (not just one), tie them into headlines, bullets, stories, and offers, and force yourself to generate many answers to each reason to discover powerful, unique angles.

## Key points
- There are at least ten primary reasons people buy: save money, save time, avoid effort, escape pain, get comfort, improve cleanliness/health, gain praise, feel loved, increase popularity/status, and (first five) make money/save money/save time/avoid effort/escape pain.
- Most sellers offer only one reason (usually make/save money). Use multiple reasons to "tie down" the prospect — the more reasons you attach, the harder it is for them to ignore or delay buying.
- Use structured questioning to uncover how your product satisfies each reason; then brainstorm many answers (force yourself beyond the obvious) to uncover unique selling bullets.

## Practical exercise (apply to your product)
- Ask targeted questions: How does this product save money? Save time? Avoid effort? Relieve pain? Increase comfort? Improve health/cleanliness? Create praise, love, popularity, or status?  
- For each question, brainstorm at least 5–10 concrete answers; push past easy ideas to discover less-obvious angles.

## Examples (illustrative)
- Protein shake: ties to saving money, saving time, avoiding effort, escaping hunger/pain, improving health, gaining praise, and social status (losing weight).  
- Executive coaching: ties to making money, saving money (vs consultants), avoiding effort (templates), escaping mental pain (work/life imbalance), gaining status/comfort.  
- Dog training: ties to saving money (avoid trainers), avoiding effort, escaping pain/liability (dog bites → lawsuits), and improving family safety.

## How to use this in copy
- Map multiple reasons to headlines, bullets, stories, and calls to action.  
- Use the list as a lens for ideation: every asset (headline, email, landing page, ad) should reference one or more strong whys.  
- Prioritize unusual or underused whys competitors ignore — those become differentiators.

## Actionable checklist for CI copy
1. Memorize the ten reasons people buy and keep them handy.  
2. For each CI offer, list which of the ten whys apply and write 5–10 specific bullets for each.  
3. Use at least 3 distinct whys in the headline + first bullets to "tie down" prospects.  
4. Run headline/bullet tests that vary the dominant "why" to see which resonates most.
