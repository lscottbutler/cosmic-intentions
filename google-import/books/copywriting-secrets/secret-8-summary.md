# Copywriting Secrets — Secret 8: Meet Fred, Your Ideal Customer

Source: user-pasted chapter (PQR2, avatar building, and the power of audience-focused copy)

## One-sentence summary
The most powerful copy speaks directly to a specific avatar by mirroring their problems, questions, roadblocks, and desired results (PQR2), and it is built by deeply understanding the exact person you are trying to move from fear to confidence.

## Key points
- Your copy must help the prospect mentally cross from where they are now to where they want to be; your messaging is the bridge.
- Cold and warm traffic are especially tuned to problems, questions, and roadblocks, so your copy must address those before presenting results.
- Fred represents your ideal customer; define a specific micro-niche and understand his thoughts, fears, frustrations, and desires better than he does.
- Do not rely only on demographics. Psychographics (what people think, want, fear, and feel) are the key to persuasive copy.
- You sell what people want, not what they need; the copy should speak to desires while the product fulfills needs.

## PQR2 framework
- Problems: what is blocking the prospect now?
- Questions: what are they asking to understand or solve?
- Roadblocks: what obstacles keep them stuck?
- Results: what outcomes do they desperately want?

## Research sources for Fred’s PQR2
- Live interaction at events: listen for complaints, pain, and questions.
- Forums: mine niche-specific forums for real audience language.
- Help desk / customer service: identify repeat issues and pain points.
- Answer sites: find real questions on Quora, Answers.yahoo.com, and similar sites.
- Surveys: use existing surveys or run your own to capture current concerns.
- Social media: ask directly in groups and monitor hashtags, comments, and trends.
- Google search: search keywords plus FAQ, mistakes, questions, top 10.
- Popular products: analyze books, products, reviews, TOCs, and feedback for market desires.

## Practical insights
- The majority of prospects are stuck on the "first cliff" of problems and doubts; your copy must show the bridge to the results they want.
- Emotional transformation matters more than money: move people from fear to security, stress to peace, pain to comfort, boredom to fun.
- Fun is a legitimate selling point; it can be a powerful differentiator in your offers.
- Narrow your audience to a micro-niche with a specific avatar (Fred the Flipper, couch potatoes, etc.) — narrower targeting yields better ad efficiency and copy resonance.

## Avatar building guidance
- Name your avatar to make them real and write directly to them.
- Start broad with niche, narrow to sub-niche, and refine to micro-niche.
- Use psychographics first, then demographic refinement.
- Create a profile that includes the words they use, their current emotional state, and their desired outcomes.

## Actionable checklist for CI copy
1. Define a named avatar for each micro-niche and write their top problems, questions, roadblocks, and desired results.  
2. For each avatar, list 5–10 items in each PQR2 category and look for the emotional fifth item.  
3. Build headlines, email subjects, bullets, and content around those exact words and pains.  
4. Use the avatar to narrow traffic targeting, messaging, and offer positioning.  
5. Focus copy on what they want, while ensuring the product delivers what they need.
